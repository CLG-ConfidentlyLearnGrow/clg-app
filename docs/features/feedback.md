# Feedback from everyone

Requested by Carla, 15 Nov 2026: "It needs to be really easy for anyone and everyone to give feedback. This feedback needs to be grouped so how each aspect of the app and of the business and of the people is progressing and how this is all performing, how it can improve and what is going well."

## 1. What it's for

- One **Feedback** link in the top bar for everyone signed in: parents, students, tutors and staff.
- One short form with four questions:
  1. What is it about? (an area of the app, an area of CLG's service, or a person at CLG)
  2. Is it praise, an idea, or a worry?
  3. An optional 1 to 5 rating.
  4. A comment.
- An overview for admins, grouped by area. It shows what's going well, ideas, and worries over the last 90 days, the average rating, and whether things are getting better compared with the 90 days before.
- Small "something felt off" worries about a colleague (UK "low-level concerns") go here, as Carla asked. Child safety worries stay on the Child safety page, and the form says so in red.

## 2. What the evidence says

- **Feedback works when it's acted on and the loop is closed.** EEF "Effective Professional Development" (2021) and EEF "Feedback" (Teaching and Learning Toolkit) both find feedback improves practice when it's specific and followed by action. The overview shows each comment with a stage (not read yet, read, being acted on, done). The person who gave it can see the stage, so they know they were heard.
- **Student and family voice.** AERO, "Student voice" and "Engaging families in learning" (2023): asking learners and families regularly, in a simple way, improves engagement and gives early warning of problems. That's why there's one short form that's always one tap away, rather than long surveys.
- **Low-level concerns.** ICMEC, "Developing and implementing a low-level concerns policy" (2023), and UK "Keeping Children Safe in Education": small worries about staff should be easy to share, kept confidential, and looked at for patterns. Feedback about a person is grouped per person, so patterns are visible to the Child Safety Officer.
- **Ratings with comments.** A rating alone tells you little. The comment box is required and the rating is optional, so the overview always explains the number.

## 3. Legal rules it touches

- **Privacy Act / APPs, and the Children's Online Privacy Code.** Only the minimum is collected: area, kind, rating and comment, plus the giver's name and role so CLG can follow up. Feedback isn't anonymous, and the page says so. The giver sees their own feedback but not the admins' private notes.
- **Feedback about a person** is seen only by the Child Safety Officer, or by the deputy when it's about the officer (Carla, 15 Nov 2026: "only me"). The person it's about never sees it. That's a database rule, and it applies even if that person is the owner. Other admins can't see it either.
- **Concerning words.** Feedback is checked like other writing, except feedback about a person. Word flags are seen by both child safety contacts, so checking that feedback would show it to the person it's about.
- **Fair Work.** Feedback about staff isn't a disciplinary record. If it leads to action, normal procedural fairness applies.

## 4. Areas

Owner-editable, and hidden areas keep their old feedback.

- **The app:** booking and sessions, progress and reports, billing and payments, learning materials, notices and emails, how easy it is to use, something else.
- **CLG's service:** tutoring sessions, being matched with a tutor, session times, communication from CLG, prices and value, other support (not tutoring), something else.
- **People at CLG:** a person (staff can choose any staff member; families can choose admins and their own tutors).

## 5. How we'll know it works

- Feedback arrives from every role in the pilot.
- Most feedback reaches "done" within a month.
- Worries in an area fall from one 90-day period to the next.
